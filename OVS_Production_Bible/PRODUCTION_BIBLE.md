# OVS PRODUCTION BIBLE
### Omnia Vanitas Studios — Master Creative Document
*Every new project starts by re-reading this.*

---

## 1. Studio Manifesto

**One line:** Omnia Vanitas Studios makes cinematic short-form content that looks expensive, feels dangerous, and means something.

**One paragraph:** OVS exists to prove that AI-assisted filmmaking can carry genuine artistic weight. We make horror-inflected, visually precise, darkly funny work rooted in an uneasy American noir sensibility — the kind of film you can't shake. "Omnia Vanitas" isn't nihilism; it's a dare. Everything fades, so make the thing worth remembering. We don't explain ourselves. We don't soften edges. We ship work that earns a second watch.

**What we make:**
Short films, micro-narratives, visual poems, mood pieces, and generative cinematic sequences — all built for screen, all carrying a distinct OVS fingerprint.

**What we do not make:**
Content. Filler. Reactionary trend-chasing. Anything that needs a caption to land.

## 2. Tonal North Star

OVS operates at the intersection of four tonal DNA strands. Every project should be consciously placed on each dial before production begins.

### The Four Levers

| Director / Studio | What They Contribute | Dial Range |
|---|---|---|
| **David Fincher** | Precision, dread-as-craft, controlled darkness, perfect sound design, the unseen detail | 0–10 |
| **Jordan Peele** | Social subtext, paranoia, the mundane surface cracking open, Black American gaze | 0–10 |
| **Quentin Tarantino** | Swagger, stylized dialogue rhythm, violence as choreography, needle-drop conviction | 0–10 |
| **A24** | Restraint, earned ambiguity, the slow burn, sensory over explanatory | 0–10 |

### How to Dial Per Project

**Default OVS blend:** Fincher 7 / Peele 5 / Tarantino 4 / A24 6

- **Horror-dominant piece:** Fincher 8, Peele 7, Tarantino 2, A24 7
- **Dark comedy piece:** Fincher 4, Peele 4, Tarantino 8, A24 3
- **Mood / visual poem:** Fincher 6, Peele 3, Tarantino 2, A24 9
- **Thriller / tension short:** Fincher 9, Peele 5, Tarantino 5, A24 5

**Rule:** If any single dial hits 10, at least one other dial must be below 4. Pure homage is not OVS.

## 3. Genre Posture

### We Are
- Horror-inflected — dread is a design element, not a genre requirement
- Darkly comedic — the laugh should arrive uncomfortable and leave a mark
- Cinematic — every frame is a decision; nothing is accidental

### We Are Not
- **Camp:** We take our own work seriously. Winking at the camera breaks the contract with the audience.
- **Torture porn:** Explicit gore without consequence or craft is not OVS. Violence earns its place or stays off screen.
- **Mumblecore:** Spontaneous, undesigned naturalism is a different tradition. OVS is controlled.
- **Shock-only:** The disturbing thing must also be beautiful or funny or true.

### The Line Test
Before any scene, sequence, or prompt batch: *Would a thoughtful version of this audience feel manipulated or respected?* If manipulated — rethink.

## 4. Visual Language

### 4a. Default Lens / Focal Length

| Situation | Focal Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Protagonist close-up | 85mm–105mm | Slight compression, face fills frame |
| Environmental tension | 40mm–50mm | "Eye" lens — implicates the viewer |
| Wide establishing | 21mm–28mm | Only when scale is the point |
| Surveillance / dread | 135mm+ | Distance as menace |
| Handheld intimacy | 35mm | Reserved — overuse = TV drama |

**Default:** 85mm. Most OVS shots live here.

### 4b. Signature Color Palette

**Primaries (always available):**
- Shadow anchor: `#0A0A0F` — near-black with a blue-violet lean
- Midtone: `#2B2420` — warm shadow, aged wood
- Highlight skin: `#C4936A` — golden amber, the one warm note in a cold frame

**Accent family (use sparingly — one per scene):**
- OVS Red: `#8B1A1A` — desaturated blood, aged velvet
- OVS Teal: `#1A4A4A` — deep water, bad fluorescent
- Bone: `#E8DCC8` — old paper, exposed skull

**Grade signature:** Crushed blacks, pulled-down highlights, slight green in the shadows, warm amber in the mids. Think *Se7en* meets *No Country for Old Men* — the world as it looked before anyone cleaned it up.

### 4c. Lighting Rules

1. **Practicals first.** Every scene should have a visible practical source — a lamp, a neon sign, a bare bulb, a phone screen. This grounds the AI generation in physical reality.
2. **Hard light is honesty, soft light is comfort.** OVS defaults to hard sources. Soft wraps are earned, not default.
3. **Shadow is compositional.** If a shadow isn't doing something — blocking something we shouldn't see yet, or pointing somewhere — it shouldn't be there.
4. **One key, one fill, no more.** Avoid the multi-lit, uniformly lit look. Baroque lighting ≠ OVS.
5. **Night > Day.** When the story can live at night, it should.

### 4d. Grain, Grade & Aspect Ratio

- **Grain:** Always present. 35mm emulation preferred. Fine grain at `ISO 800–1600` equivalent. Grain should be *visible at 100% zoom* but not distracting at viewing size.
- **Grade:** Apply after generation. Never rely on AI output color as final.
- **Aspect ratio default:** 2.39:1 (anamorphic widescreen). Use 1.85:1 for more intimate work. Never 16:9 unless the brief specifically requires it.
- **Vignette:** Subtle — present at edges, absent at center. Not a filter effect; a focal truth.

## 5. Sound & Score Philosophy

Sound is 50% of the experience. OVS pieces without sound design are unfinished.

### When Score Enters
Score earns its entrance. Default behavior: **scene starts without score.** Score enters only when:
- The emotional moment has already arrived and score amplifies what's already true
- A tonal pivot requires marking
- The scene is a pure mood piece where score is the grammar

**Never:** Score to explain what visuals already show.

### Needle-Drop Logic
OVS needle-drops are **unexpected decade, perfect emotional fit.** The song should feel wrong for a half-second, then devastatingly right. Rules:
- No contemporary pop unless deeply ironic
- 50s–70s strongly preferred
- The lyric should have a second reading in context

### Silence as Tool
Silence is an active choice, not an absence. Use it:
- Before the worst thing happens
- After the funny thing lands (let the audience sit in it)
- When the character knows something the audience doesn't yet

### Diegetic vs. Score
Prefer **diegetic sound** in realistic scenes — radio, TV bleed, traffic, ambient environment. Score is for the interior.

## 6. Character Archetypes

### 6a. The Stylish Protagonist

OVS leads are always slightly out of place in their own world — overdressed, over-aware, or dangerously composed. They move like they've already seen how this ends.

**Wardrobe defaults:**
- Dark, well-fitted, slightly vintage — never loud
- One wrong detail (a stain, a button undone, shoes that don't match the rest)
- No sports logos, no casual contemporary branding

**Casting language for prompts:**
> "A man/woman in their late 30s to mid-40s, lean face, watchful eyes, impeccably dressed in [era/palette], standing still while everything around them moves."

**Speech rhythms:** Economical. Pauses more than speaks. Never explains themselves. The first thing they say is never what they mean.

### 6b. The Antagonist

Not a monster — a system, a force, or a person who's made their peace with something the protagonist hasn't. Often polite. Frequently well-dressed. **The horror is how reasonable they seem.**

### 6c. Recurring Supporting Types

| Type | Role | Wardrobe Signal |
|---|---|---|
| The Witness | Sees everything, says nothing | Institutional uniform or nondescript |
| The Believer | Has crossed a line the protagonist hasn't | Faded wealth — once had more |
| The Child | Uncanny. Knows the rules of the wrong world | Clean but wrong — too clean |
| The Functionary | The machine made human | Polyester, name tag, no eye contact |

## 7. World Rules & Motifs

### 7a. Vintage Americana Surface

The OVS world looks like America between 1955 and 1985, filtered through a bad dream. The cars are beautiful, the buildings are decaying, the signs still glow. This is not nostalgia — it's an autopsy.

**Required era details in establishing shots:**
- American-made vehicles (see 7c)
- Incandescent and neon — no LED strips
- Physical signage — hand-painted or vintage printed
- Rotary phones, analog gauges, glass bottles

### 7b. Supernatural Intrusion Logic

OVS does not explain its supernatural elements. Rules:
1. **It was always there.** The supernatural isn't an arrival — it's a revelation. It was present before frame one.
2. **One intrusion per piece.** Don't stack. One impossible thing, completely committed.
3. **No rules dialogue.** Characters do not explain how the supernatural thing works. They react.
4. **Visual rhyme.** The supernatural element should visually echo something ordinary in the same piece — the shadow that matches the lamp, the figure that mirrors the mannequin.

### 7c. The Cadillac

The vintage Cadillac is the OVS signature vehicle — the 1959 Series 62, the 1966 DeVille, the 1973 Eldorado. It represents:
- American decay dressed as elegance
- The thing that's survived past its context
- The protagonist's relationship with the past

**In prompts:** "A black [year] Cadillac [model], polished but aged, parked under [light source], [weather condition]."

### 7d. Recurring Objects & Motifs

| Object | Symbolic Register |
|---|---|
| Rotary telephone | Unfinished conversation, the past calling |
| Bare light bulb (swinging) | Exposure, interrogation, the truth that hurts |
| Mirror (cracked or whole) | Identity fracture, the watching self |
| Playing cards | Fate, game, the fixed outcome |
| Cigarette smoke | Time passing, the body consuming itself |
| Rain on glass | Separation, observation, the world filtered |
| An open door (dark beyond) | Choice, threshold, the point of no return |

### 7e. Weather & Location Defaults

- **Weather:** Overcast default. Rain second. Clear skies only in ironic counterpoint.
- **Locations:** Interior spaces with one exterior view. Parking lots at night. Roadside establishments. Empty diners. Motel corridors.
- **Season:** Late autumn or deep winter. The world stripped down.

## 8. The OVS Prompt Anatomy

All Seedance 2.0 video prompts follow this structure. Every element is required unless explicitly noted as optional.

```
[LENS] [SUBJECT] [ACTION/STATE] [ENVIRONMENT] [LIGHTING] [GRADE/TEXTURE] [REFERENCE VECTOR] -- [NEGATIVE PROMPT]
```

### Element Definitions

**[LENS]** — Focal length and camera behavior
> `85mm medium close-up, locked camera` / `40mm slow push in` / `135mm telephoto, stationary`

**[SUBJECT]** — Who or what, with specific visual descriptors
> `A man in his mid-40s, lean, wearing a dark wool suit, white shirt open at collar`

**[ACTION/STATE]** — What they're doing or how they exist in the frame
> `standing at a window, not looking out, cigarette burning down in his right hand`

**[ENVIRONMENT]** — Location, time, atmosphere
> `a motel room, late 1960s, overcast daylight through thin curtains, single lamp on`

**[LIGHTING]** — Dominant source, quality, color temperature
> `practical lamplight warm amber, secondary cold window light, hard shadow on wall`

**[GRADE/TEXTURE]** — Film stock, grain, color character
> `35mm grain, crushed blacks, muted palette, slight green shadow push`

**[REFERENCE VECTOR]** — Tonal reference (film title, photographer, era)
> `in the visual register of No Country for Old Men, Gordon Parks photography`

**[NEGATIVE PROMPT]** — What must not appear (see Section 9)

### Example Full Prompt

```
85mm medium close-up, slow rack focus from hands to face — A man in his mid-40s, lean jaw, 
dark wool suit, white shirt open at the collar — standing at a motel room window, not looking 
out, a cigarette burning in his right hand — late 1960s motel room, thin curtain diffusing 
flat overcast daylight, one practical table lamp — practical warm amber dominant, hard wall 
shadow, cool secondary fill from window — 35mm grain visible, crushed blacks, muted warm 
palette, subtle green shadow cast — visual register of No Country for Old Men, Richard Avedon 
portraiture — no digital sheen, no modern clothing, no HDR look, no stock footage lighting, 
no visible AI artifacts, no lens flare, no oversaturated color
```

## 9. Standard Negative Prompts

The de-AI-ification list. Include relevant lines in every prompt batch.

### Visual Artifacts
```
no visible AI artifacts, no morphing geometry, no extra fingers, no limb doubling,
no face flicker, no texture swimming, no background warping
```

### Wrong Aesthetic
```
no HDR look, no oversaturated color, no color grading presets, no stock footage lighting,
no ring light, no beauty dish fill, no lifestyle photography look
```

### Wrong Era / World
```
no modern clothing, no digital devices, no LED lights, no contemporary vehicles,
no brand logos, no plastic surfaces unless period-appropriate
```

### Wrong Tone
```
no horror jump-scare framing, no comedy pratfall staging, no music video aesthetics,
no fashion editorial posing, no social media vertical framing
```

### Camera / Craft
```
no shaky handheld unless specified, no drone shot unless specified,
no slow-motion unless specified, no text overlays, no subtitles in frame
```

## 10. Project Pipeline

Every OVS project — regardless of scale — passes through these gates in order. No gate is skipped.

```
BRIEF → TREATMENT → SHOT LIST → PROMPT BATCH → GENERATION → ASSEMBLY → GRADE → RELEASE
```

### Gate Definitions

**BRIEF** (`08_Templates/brief_template.md`)
One page. Premise, tone dials, format, length, target platform, one-line that must be true at the end.

**TREATMENT** (`08_Templates/treatment_template.md`)
Scene-by-scene narrative description. No shots yet — just what happens and what it feels like. Include tonal dial settings here.

**SHOT LIST** (`08_Templates/shot_list_template.md`)
Shot number, framing, lens, action, audio note. No prompts yet — this is still craft language.

**PROMPT BATCH** (`06_Prompt_Library/`)
Shot list translated into OVS Prompt Anatomy format. One `.md` file per scene. Includes negative prompt block.

**GENERATION**
Seedance 2.0 runs. Outputs go to `10_Assets/[project_name]/raw_gen/`. Never curate during generation — generate all, curate after.

**ASSEMBLY**
Select takes, arrange timeline. Document selects in shot list with take numbers. Rough cut to brief.

**GRADE**
Apply OVS grade signature. Check against palette. Verify grain. Confirm aspect ratio.

**RELEASE**
Passes Quality Bar Checklist (Section 11). Archive brief + prompts + selects to `07_Project_Archive/_SHIPPED/`. Post.

## 11. Quality Bar Checklist

A finished piece must pass all of these before it ships under the OVS name. Answer honestly — not aspirationally.

1. **The Fincher test:** Is every visible element in every frame deliberate? Is anything there by accident?
2. **The Peele test:** Is there a subtext reading? Does the piece mean something beyond what it shows?
3. **The silence test:** Does the piece use at least one moment of silence as an active tool, not a gap?
4. **The Cadillac test:** Is there at least one vintage Americana visual that grounds the world?
5. **The second-watch test:** Is there something a second viewer would notice that a first viewer missed?
6. **The stranger test:** Could you show this to someone who knows nothing about OVS and they'd feel the tonal signature?
7. **The camp line:** Does anything in the piece wink at the camera or undercut its own premise for cheap effect?
8. **The grain test:** Is grain present and visible? Is the grade OVS palette — not default AI output?
9. **The negative prompt test:** Are there any visible AI artifacts, modern anachronisms, or wrong-era elements?
10. **The one-line test:** Can you state in one sentence what this piece is *about* — not what happens in it, but what it means?

**Threshold:** A piece shipping with two or more failures gets shelved, not patched. A shelf is not failure — it's integrity.
